Krigsvold publishes interpretation, not original scientific research. Its job
is to make established evidence understandable without making it look more
certain, more current, or more comprehensive than it is.

## Source hierarchy

Briefings prefer, in order:

1. direct observations and datasets from the scientific institution responsible
   for them;
2. peer-reviewed research articles;
3. assessments from bodies such as the IPCC and the Scientific Committee on
   Antarctic Research; and
4. explanatory material from national Antarctic programs and public science
   agencies.

Secondary reporting may help identify a subject, but a central factual claim
should lead readers to the underlying research or responsible institution.

## How claims are written

Observed change, modeled projection, and policy judgment are identified as
different things. Measurements include a period. Species status names the
listing authority. Estimates retain words such as “about” when precision would
be false.

Each briefing carries a review date. Review means that its central claims and
links were checked on that date; it does not mean that every cited dataset was
released then.

## Corrections

Minor typographical fixes are made in place. Corrections that change the meaning
of a scientific, programmatic, or institutional claim are logged on the
[Corrections](/corrections/) page with the date and substance of the change.

Questions about a source or correction can be sent to
[count@krigsvold.org](mailto:count@krigsvold.org).
