Ria Raut is a doctoral student in Environmental Science, Policy, and Management
at the University of California, Berkeley. She received the 2026 Fellowship for
research into the recovery of selectively logged tropical forests.

## The research

Working in the Danum Valley Conservation Area and Ulu-Segama Forest Reserve,
Raut compares sapling recruitment in primary and regenerating forest. Her
project asks whether differences between logged and unlogged stands are driven
principally by biological factors—such as adult tree species and mycorrhizal
communities—or by physical conditions including soil biochemistry and canopy
light.

The answer matters because restoration is not simply a matter of waiting for
trees to return. A forest's future composition depends on which seedlings
survive, what conditions favor them, and how logging has changed those
conditions. Better evidence about recruitment can improve restoration
strategies in one of the world's most biodiverse and heavily pressured
ecosystems.

## Why the Fellowship supported it

The project combines field observation with a clear conservation application.
It addresses carbon storage, biodiversity, and ecological resilience while
producing knowledge that land managers can use. That combination of analytical
rigor and practical consequence is exactly what the Fellowship exists to
support.
