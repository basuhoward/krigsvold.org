Three dignities held by James P. Howard, II correspond to three named features
in Marie Byrd Land: the **Krigsvold Nunataks**, **Mount Rubin de la Borbolla**,
and **Matikonis Peak**.

The association supplies Krigsvold with a geography. It does not supply a
territory. The titles convey no ownership, sovereignty, jurisdiction, or
authority over these places.

## Krigsvold Nunataks

The [Krigsvold Nunataks](https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/gaz/display_name.cfm?gaz_id=127606)
are a group of exposed rocky peaks at the head of Strauss Glacier. A nunatak is
rock that rises above the surrounding ice sheet: a fixed point visible in a
landscape defined by movement.

The feature was named for **Sergeant Alvin I. Krigsvold**, following the
Antarctic practice of commemorating people associated with exploration and its
support. It is the geographic basis of the principal dignity and the source of
this site's name.

Krigsvold's arms—*Azure, a snowflake Argent, a chief invected Argent*—render
that geography in heraldic shorthand: ice, polar blue, and an irregular ridge
breaking through the surface.

## Mount Rubin de la Borbolla

[Mount Rubin de la Borbolla](https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/gaz/display_name.cfm?gaz_id=131013)
is a mountain in Marie Byrd Land named in 1974 by the Advisory Committee on
Antarctic Names for **George S. Rubin de la Borbolla**, a meteorologist who
served at Plateau Station in 1968.

The name is a reminder that Antarctic knowledge depends on long chains of
observation: instruments maintained, records continued, and people working in
conditions that make routine measurement anything but routine.

## Matikonis Peak

[Matikonis Peak](https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/gaz/display_name.cfm?gaz_id=128543)
stands in the Coulter Heights. It was named for **William P. Matikonis** and is
the feature associated with the dignity Baron of Matikonis.

Like other nunataks and peaks, it makes visible the meeting of ice and rock. It
is not evidence of a private claim; it is a named place through which this
office keeps its work connected to the actual Antarctic map.

## Why the places matter here

Remote advocacy can become vague remarkably quickly. These three names resist
that tendency. They require the institution to return to location, measurement,
history, and the limits of its own claims. That is useful discipline.
